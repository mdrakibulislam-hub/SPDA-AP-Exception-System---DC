What to deliver

They weight the assessment roughly 75% UX and product thinking and 25% UI execution. Keep it low fidelity and go deep on one workflow. You can skip user research, a full design system, and screens outside your chosen scope.

1. One Figma file

Share a view link with comment access. Make these four pages.

  1. Cover. Your name, date, what’s on each page, and a short dated log of important decisions.
  2. Thinking. The problem, assumptions, and end-to-end flow across roles with today’s pain points. Show the records the system tracks, their statuses, and who can see or do what.
  3. Core flow. Use the PDF’s sample data in 3–5 key screens at 1440 px wide. Show how an approver or supplier takes part as the second role.
  4. Prototype. Make one screen from that flow fully interactive in Figma, by hand and without AI tools. Every control should work. Include clicks, inputs, hover, focus, pressed, disabled and error states, and transitions as applicable.

Use your own component assets, name frames and layers clearly, and mark the final frames.

Original brief, pages 3–4

How the system works

Design a first version of a tool that helps SPDA Supply’s finance team fix supplier invoices that get stuck, so they can pay the right supplier the right amount sooner.

This is an accounts payable (AP) invoice exception workflow. Accounts payable is the team that handles bills from suppliers. An exception is a bill someone needs to investigate or approve before it can move forward. About 880 of 4,000 invoices get stuck each month across the US, UK, and Bangladesh.

  1. Read and compare. AI reads the supplier’s invoice. The existing process compares what the supplier billed with what SPDA ordered (the purchase order or PO) and what arrived (the goods receipt).
  2. Match or flag. About 78% match and are posted automatically to the existing finance system (the ERP). The other 22% need human attention.
  3. Investigate and decide. Today AP specialists work from a shared inbox and spreadsheet, re-enter data in the ERP, and chase approvals by email. Your proposed flow should show how they find the issue, gather evidence, and get a decision from the right person.
  4. Move forward or stay on hold. The ERP remains the official record for orders, receipts, and payments. Show how people can tell where an exception stands, including when a decision, new evidence, or an ERP update is still pending.

Example from the brief

Northgate Packaging’s invoice 88213 bills for 60 units, but the receipt covers 40. The AI score is 0.97, so it read the invoice with high confidence. An AP specialist still needs to investigate the 20-unit gap because quantities must match exactly.

Original brief, pages 2–3

One way to scope Phase 1

Start with the AP specialist and use the budget approver as the second role. The specialist handles the queue all day on a desktop. The approver makes occasional decisions, often on a phone. Controllers care about audit and month-end close, and suppliers want to know when they’ll be paid. Show where those roles fit in your flow and choose how much of their experience to design.

Follow one invoice all the way through. You could focus on a price or quantity mismatch, which makes up 41% of exceptions. Show the specialist finding the problem, comparing the evidence, resolving it or routing it to the right person, and seeing the result. Choose a case that makes the second role’s contribution clear.

Write down what the tool needs to track. For example, link an invoice to its exception and any approval request. Give the exception statuses like “Needs review” and “Waiting for evidence,” and say who can move it forward. Think through what happens when an approver is away or the ERP fails to sync.

Decide where to save clicks. The AP lead wants faster bulk work. The controller needs decisions and edits to remain auditable, with approval and bank verification rules enforced.

Business goals to measure

  • Average time to resolve an exception drops from 9 days to 3.
  • Duplicate or fraudulent payments drop to zero. Last quarter there were two duplicates totaling $38,400.
  • Month-end close drops from 8 to 5 working days.
  • Supplier status inquiries fall from about 300 to 150 per month.
